# Monetary Transmission

Monetary transmission is the process linking policy decisions to financial conditions, spending and prices. Channels include bank lending, expectations, asset prices and exchange rates. The timing and strength of the effects vary. [ECB: Transmission mechanism](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/intro/transmission/html/index.en.html)

Distinguish an **announced action** from a **surprise relative to expectations**. Financial markets can react before implementation; a higher policy rate can coexist with lower long-term yields if expectations change.

For a concrete claim, ask: which rate changed, whose financing changed, what spending response followed, and over what period? A diagram alone does not establish the magnitude of any effect.

## Relationships

- **influences → [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|Inflation]]** — Changes in financing conditions and spending can affect inflation. The effect has variable lags; no fixed timetable or guaranteed size is implied. [ECB: Transmission mechanism](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/intro/transmission/html/index.en.html) ^rates-rel-11

- **influences_via_yields → [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bond Prices and Yields|Bond Prices and Yields]]** — Policy and expectations can change market yields and consequently bond valuations. Long-term yields need not move one-for-one with the current policy rate. [ECB: Transmission mechanism](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/mopo/intro/transmission/html/index.en.html) ^rates-rel-12
