# Why is Gold Crashing from the War?

There's been a ton of confusion recently about this, the recent crash in the price of gold despite the fact that we have geopolitical chaos. We have wars developing. We have oil spiking. People are wondering what in the world is going on. What shenanigans are taking place?

Gold is down 25% basically from the start of the war until now. What is going on? Why in the world is gold selling off and crashing when it looks like stuff is going on that should be making gold do the opposite. Gold should be going through the roof.

I'm going to answer that. It comes down to a couple of different things happening in the market right now. Number one, and most importantly, this is a liquidity crunch. So, there's an old saying that says when there's a liquidity crisis, you don't sell what you want to sell, you sell what you can sell.

And that is exactly what is going on right now. Number one, everything has sold off. Obviously, gold is down 25% almost from the start of the war until the bottom just put in today as the time of this recording. [[Finance/07 Crypto and Digital Money/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]] is fairly flat during that time frame.

[[Finance/08 Gold Silver and Commodities/Silver|Silver's]] down a ton. The S&P 500 is down a ton. Even [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Treasury Securities and Issuance|treasuries]] are down a ton. TLT is down almost 5% from the start of the war. Really, the only thing that's up is oil and the dollar. And so, anybody who needs liquidity is selling everything, selling what they can.

And globally, especially [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Central Banks|central banks]] and sovereign nations, this means gold is going to get sold off. This is actually one of the functions of gold being a reserve asset. It functions like a reserve asset, meaning you pull on it in times of need. This is one of the reasons why I talk about a 20% gold allocation in your portfolio because in times of need, you want to be able to have something to draw.

You want to have some dry powder. That is exactly how this is functioning right now. And so because gold is functioning like a global reserve asset, it is getting sold off so that all the big holders of gold have some liquidity to be able to use. Now this is bleeding over into the second point which is that it is a global reserve asset.

This is a chart that shows the gold holdings as a share of official foreign reserve assets compared to US debt like [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Treasury Securities and Issuance|treasuries]]. And you can see over the last couple of years, gold has overtaken US [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Treasury Securities and Issuance|treasuries]] in terms of the percentages of foreign reserve holdings. And so nations that need to be able to make payments, they're going to draw on their reserves. That's going to be [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Treasury Securities and Issuance|treasuries]] and it's going to be gold.

So gold is down and [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Treasury Securities and Issuance|treasuries]] are down. Now, this is another reason why I always talk about having some cash in the reserves portion of your portfolio as well. I usually talk about like 5% because as they say, you never want to put all of your hell in one hand basket. The dollar being up during a time like now was not on anybody's radar.

I mean, I know if you've been watching my channel about a year ago, I started saying that I was pretty bullish on the dollar, the DXY index specifically, which is the dollar versus other currencies. And I said I thought it would very soon come back above that 100 level. And it looks like we are on our way during a time period where everybody would have expected the opposite would have been happening, especially because gold had such a positive run up before this war started. And that brings me to the third reason why gold is selling off right now.

That's specifically because what it has already done. Rise of gold from March 2024 to the top that took place in January was a 166% rise. So gold went up about 2 and 1/2x in the course of 2 years. That's what a blowoff top looks like.

When you see a really healthy bull market, consolidation with a wedge, another breakout, really healthy bull market, and then you see this price action where the price just explodes and then single day you put in big wicks on your candles and everything collapses within a couple of days and the price falls 21% within the course of just two trading days. That's what a blowoff top looks like. And from this moment, I said I thought the bull market was probably done. And yes, gold did make its way back up almost to the previous all-time highs there.

But even the start of geopolitical chaos and war was not enough to get this thing to go up. In fact, people started drawing on it for payments and it started to fall. If you've been following my channel for a while, you know that I've been saying I didn't think that the gold would sustain above 5,000 for a long time. Long-term, I'm bullish on gold.

I'm not selling it right now. I'm not trading it. But the insane rise that we've had in gold over the last two years means that at some point that buying is going to bleed out. That's going to run dry. It's going to hit the end of its rally.

And the reason for that is because number four, the fourth reason why gold is selling off right now. Gold tends to anticipate [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Monetary Policy|monetary policy]] well in advance. The people who do the majority of the gold buying in the world are the [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Central Banks|central banks]]. The people who do the planning and the setting of [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Monetary Policy|monetary policy]] are [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Central Banks|central banks]].

This is why gold tends to make its moves in advance of [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Monetary Policy|monetary policy]]. If you see big easing coming down the road, you're going to see gold move up ahead of that. If you see tightening coming down the road, you're going to see gold sell off ahead of that. A great example of this is how gold started a huge bull market in June of 2019, well in advance of all the money printing that was set to take place in early 2020.

Gold skyrocketed and then really once the money printing just was getting started, gold had already peaked in August of 2020. gold moved sideways and sold off from there for a couple of years as it started to price in the tightening that was coming from the [[Finance/02 Inflation and Purchasing Power/Inflation|inflation]] that [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Central Banks|central banks]] had unleashed. And then it wasn't until 2024 when gold broke out again in anticipation of the future easing and future printing that was coming down the pipeline. But now gold has fully priced in all of the easing that is coming in and start it will be coming in very soon here.

We see bank deregulation getting talked about. We see [[Finance/04 Bonds and Funding Markets/Yield Curves|yield curve]] control getting talked about. we see [[Finance/04 Bonds and Funding Markets/Interest and Interest Rates|interest rate]] cuts getting talked about and obviously some of that is volatile right now because if there is [[Finance/02 Inflation and Purchasing Power/Inflation|inflation]] from the war from oil going up then there's going to be some things on hold which is again why we're having this selloff here because if gold has to start pricing in tightening from [[Finance/02 Inflation and Purchasing Power/Inflation|inflation]] then the easing that it priced in might have been too aggressive. So again the fourth reason why gold is selling off right now is because gold anticipates [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Monetary Policy|monetary policy]].

The fifth reason why gold is selling off hard right now is actually a sign that the market does not expect this conflict to be extremely longived. Back in 1979, there was another thing going on with the global oil markets and Iran. Specifically, that was the Iranian revolution, which sparked the world's second oil shock in 5 years. These strikes began in 1978.

And by January of 1979, crude oil production had declined by almost 5 million barrels per day, which was about 7% of world production at that time. And though there was some volatility in gold prices, overall, gold rose during that time. And if you follow gold price further forward and see what it did over the following years, you see gold continued its massive rise. So in short, the market is not pricing in the chance that this is going to be an extremely extended massive global issue that's going to cause huge problems for the long term.
