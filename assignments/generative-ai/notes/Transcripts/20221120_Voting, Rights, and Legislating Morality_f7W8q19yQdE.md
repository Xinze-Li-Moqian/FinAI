# Voting, Rights, and Legislating Morality

With the election going on right now, many people are concerned about who is coming into power, who will be trying to come into power in the future, and it's because most people are concerned about what new laws might pass, what that might do to change people's lives. And so, it seemed like a good thing to go over here some of the fundamental reasons behind voting and laws, what they're supposed to do, what they actually do right now, and we do also unfortunately have to get into the nitty-gritty of what human rights actually are because, contrary to how they're being used and promoted and abused right now and manipulated, rights may not actually be what you think they are.

Now, before we start off here, I do have to make a huge disclaimer at the beginning because I know that this conversation is going to be extremely triggering, very controversial. Many people are going to vehemently disagree with a lot of the things that I'm saying here. So, I need to get this out in the open first so that we can kind of move emotion to the side because a lot of what I'm going to say is going to be emotionally triggering. So, we need to move that off to the side here, just look at the facts and just look at the logic to see if the arguments hold up by themselves.

I strongly believe that it is a moral obligation for people to take care of those who cannot take care of themselves. That is why I personally give a significant amount of my income away every single month to local organizations like food pantries, homeless shelters that I know that the money is going to actually help the people who are in need and not just, you know, raise money, fancy fundraisers just to, you know, get more salaries paid for. So, I strongly believe that it is a moral obligation for individuals to choose to give away their own resources, their own money when they can't take care of those who are in need.

So, now that the feelings are out of the way, let's look at the facts. The only reason people care about voting is because laws are used to take away people's rights and not to protect them. Bam. That is a heavy statement, and I bet you most people are going to disagree about that.

So, I highly recommend you read this book, The Law by Frédéric Bastiat, because he will explain it a lot more eloquently than I'm going to talk about here, but I'm going to try and summarize it as succinctly as possible. Humans have an innate inherent right to protect their lives, their bodies, their property, and those of others. That is not a right given to us by governments. That is not a right given to us by other individuals.

That is right that humans have inherently. Therefore, when we form a collective society and create laws, the only thing that those laws are supposed to be is the collective enforcement of individual rights. Now, we all know that my rights end where your rights begin. I have the right to do literally anything I want, fundamentally, logically speaking, as long as it doesn't prevent you from doing anything you want or infringe on your body, your life, your property.

So, let's do a thought experiment here. Let's press a magic button and say that the only thing the law can be used for is protecting individual rights. If that was the case, would you care about voting? Would you care about who is in power?

No, not at all because at the end of the day, you know that the only thing the law could be used for is to protect your rights, not to infringe on your rights, take your property, infringe on your freedom. There would be no reason for you to care whatsoever about who is in power or what laws are being passed because we pressed the magic button, and the only thing laws can do is protect individual rights. The law is just the collective enforcement of the individual rights that are already there. Now, let's flip it, and we press the other magic button to see the other side of the reality that we face right now.

The only reason that people care about voting and who's in power is precisely because laws are used to infringe on rights. So, people need to make sure that the person in power isn't going to come after them, and some people care about the person in charge so that they can go after others. Two wolves and one sheep voting on what to have for dinner. And so, because the law is used to infringe on rights, people have to care about voting and have to care about who's in power, have to care about what laws are being passed so that they can make sure it's not used against them, and if it's going to be used against somebody, it's used against somebody else for my own benefit.

But now, we have to take it one step further and ask the question, well, what is actually a right? And while it's most likely impossible to produce an exhaustive list of all the rights in all detail, what we can do is draw a line and say, okay, anything that crosses this line is not a right, clearly. And this is where I'm going to lose some people here, and I know, so let me know in the comments what you think, and not just emotion, but some actual arguments that disagree with what I'm saying here.

If it requires the labor of another human being, it cannot be considered a right. I'll say it again. If that thing requires somebody else's labor, it cannot be considered a human right. Well, why is that? Let's take a look at some examples. Let's take a look at something like sunlight or breathable air.

These are things that are in extreme absolute abundance that do not require any human labor in order to access. Therefore, you might be able to make an argument that it is within human's rights to be able to see the sunlight or have the sunlight or have access to be able to, you know, breathe air. Clean breathable air is something that does happen in absolute abundance just absolutely naturally. In fact, it takes human labor to turn air in an environment like a city into non-breathable air like through pollution.

Now, let's take something like having a doctor tell you what's wrong with you and prescribe something that will fix you. Let's take something like food, something to eat, eating at a restaurant, getting food from a grocery store, clothes, shelter, like a place to live, housing. All of these things require human labor. Houses do not occur naturally in nature.

Food does kind of occur naturally in nature, but not in absolute abundance. It takes a massive enormous amount of human labor to produce enough food for everybody on Earth to be able to have enough to eat. And newsflash, there actually is enough food on Earth for everybody to eat. It's the infrastructure problem that prevents that food from getting to where it needs to go.

It's not an absolute amount of food problem. Further than that, all land is owned by somebody, and all farm animals and all agriculture is owned by somebody. It's private property, and those people who own it put in the work and the capital and the resources to produce it and produce food that they're able to sell to other people. And if you want to know a horror story about what happens when a society demonizes the people who own the [[Finance/16 Economic Reasoning/Production|means of production]] and decides to take it all away, go read The Gulag Archipelago.

So, we know that food is something that requires human labor to produce. So, could we say that having food to eat is a human right? No, we can't cuz let's say for any reason you are in a situation where you don't have food to eat tonight. Saying that it is a human right would mean that the government would enforce that you have food to eat, which would mean they would have to go take the private property of another or force human labor to produce something and then take it to give it to you.

Why is that a problem? Because something cannot be human right if it violates another human right. And the government can only take private property away from an individual or enforce labor in order to make somebody produce something that is then given to somebody else through violence. Now, you might be thinking, well, that's preposterous.

If the government tells somebody to do something and they do it, there's no violence there. But that's not actually true. The only reason you pay your [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Taxation|taxes]] is not because you believe the government is doing the best thing possible with your money, and you think that the government should have more of your money. No, you pay [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Taxation|taxes]] because if you don't, you go to jail.

Violent men with weapons show up at your house, violently arrest you, and take you to jail, and keep you there against your will. So, that's why you pay [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Taxation|taxes]]. It is because the government has a monopoly on violence, and just the threat of that violence counts. So, it doesn't matter if it's used or not, it matters that the threat of it is there because they carry the monopoly on it.

So, if they say, "Produce food and give it to this person or else," that's still through the use of violence. So, it's still violating human rights in order to enforce another one. Therefore, one of those cannot be a human right. And so, at the end of the day, if a right requires human labor, then that right's enforcement will simultaneously violate other human rights, and therefore, it itself cannot be a right.

Now, I need to make this abundantly clear because I talked about this in the beginning, but I need to reinforce this. I think that individuals have a moral obligation to choose to take care of people who can't take care of themselves, widows, orphans, people in poverty, the homeless. But just because something is morally good does not mean that it should be legislated. Just as just because something is morally evil does not mean that it should be outlawed.

You cannot legislate morality. And if you study history, you'll find that every time you try to legislate morality, you end up producing a worse situation. At the end of the day, the foundation that made this country so prosperous and laid the seeds for such immense growth, historically an anomaly, was the freedom that allowed people to do what they wanted to do as long as they didn't infringe on the rights of others. And the more we try to impose violations of rights and pretend that they are rights themselves, the more we erode that foundation and sow the seeds for our future collapse instead of future prosperity.

And one last note, just in case you think that it's necessary to use violence in order to enforce taking care of people, the ends never justify the means. And almost everybody that I know gives to charity in some form and would give much more if they weren't already losing half of their income to the government. And if you think that the government is doing charitable work with all that money that's going to them, I've got a bridge to sell you. As always, I really appreciate you guys.

Thank you so much for watching. Have a great day.
