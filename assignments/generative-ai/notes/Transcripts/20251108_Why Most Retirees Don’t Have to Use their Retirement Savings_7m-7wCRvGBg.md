# Why Most Retirees Don’t Have to Use their Retirement Savings

Most retirees die without ever having to touch their retirement savings and that's because you are paying for their retirement. We take a look at some data reported by of dollarsendata.com. We can see that 26% of retirees are withdrawing up to the amount that their portfolio earns. So of all the retirement savings that you have invested, it's going to spit off some interest and growth each year.

And over one quarter of retirees are only taking just the amount that their portfolio grows by every single year, which means they are not touching the principal at all. But an astonishing 58% of all retirees are actually withdrawing less than their investments earn. In other words, if you've got a million-doll retirement portfolio between all of your investments and it grows by, let's say, $100,000 per year, these retirees, over half, the majority of them, are withdrawing less than it actually grows by. So, less than that 100 grand in this example.

This means that a staggering 86% of all retirees in the United States never have to draw down on their own wealth. This means that from the time somebody retires to the time they die, typically their wealth is actually still increasing. A United income study showed that the average retired adult who dies in their 60s leaves behind $296,000 in wealth. But if they don't die till their 70s, they leave behind more, $313,000.

And if they die in their 80s, they leave behind even more, 315,000 on average. And it's not until you get to the people who live into their 90s that they start to actually draw that down, leaving behind an average of 238,000. Which means that the majority of American retirees have to use so little of their own wealth in retirement that their wealth actually continues to grow until they die. Now, you might be looking at numbers like this of 296,000 or 313,000.

And you might be thinking that is not enough to retire on. How are these people not needing to withdraw enough from their retirement that it's actually still growing? And the answer is because they don't have to pay for their own expenses. Taxpayers do through social security and Medicare.

These are not social safety nets. These are wealth transfer systems from poor, younger working people to older, rich, or retired non-working people. They're not needs-based. They're age- based. And age happens to be a basic onetoone correlation with wealth. If you take a look at the median net worth by age group, you can see it is a direct correlation.

The older people get, the wealthier they get. And that is reasonable because the older you are, the more earning potential you have, the more skills that you have, the more experience that you have, and the more time you have to accumulate and invest and grow that wealth. And again, if you're looking at the age cohort of 65 to 74 with a median net worth of around $400,000, and you're thinking that's not enough to retire on, it is when you have taxpayers funding your retirement for you. And I need to pause here and clarify.

I am not blaming retired people, old people. I am not resentful towards them. I do not think it is their fault. They did not choose this system. This system was around before many of them started working. The fact is that these systems are not social safety nets.

They are not benefits from something that you have paid into over your working career. Money was taken from you and paid to somebody else. They lied to you. You were not investing in anything. You were not paying into a fund. You were not storing money away and then then paying it back to you.

The only way that retirees are currently getting any of these benefits is because money is being taken from people who are less well off. And the unfortunate reality here is that this system is very quickly approaching a place where it might fall off a cliff because social security is bankrupt. There is less money going in than there is going out. There is a trust fund with a pool that is being depleted year after year and within seven years it will be empty.

And unfortunately the deadline for when the social security fund will be empty is approaching faster and faster not slower. The most recent update from this year shows that the trust fund will be depleted one year sooner than they originally thought last year. The Social Security trust fund will be depleted in 2033 and at that time 77% of the benefits will be payable. In other words, for every $100 that the government is paying out to current retired people, there are only $77 the government is taking from current workers.

The only reason they are able to still pay that $100 to current retirees right now is because there is some left over from back decades ago when the demographics were flipped and there were more working people to take money from than retired people to give that money to. But this is not a new problem. This is not something that we haven't known about for a long time. This is something that has been known would happen for decades.

And in fact, the deadline has not been just moving further away like we've been kicking the can down the road. the deadline has actually been approaching sooner, faster, quicker. This is a report from Social Security released all the way back in 2010. And at that time, they thought that the Social Security fund would not run out until 2037.

So, as time has gone on, not only are we approaching the drop deadad date sooner, but that drop deadad date has gotten closer and closer. It's been moving backwards in time. We originally thought it wouldn't happen until 2037. Now, we know it's going to happen closer to 2033.

That is in 7 years from now. Just for comparison, 2020 was 5 years ago and in a little over 5 years from now, the social security fund will be empty. So the question is what is going to happen when this social security fund is depleted? And there is no good answer because unfortunately this is not a social safety net.

This is not a system that cares for the people who can't care for themselves. This is a system that, like it or hate it, is a direct wealth transfer system that takes money from younger, poorer people who actually work and it just gives it to people who are older and richer and don't work. And so it seems the morally fair answer would be to just end the program altogether. Maybe replace it with something that is means or needs-based so that if there are seniors who are actually dependent on it that they don't die.

We already have systems in place that are needs-based and means tested like supplemental security income and food stamps. This is not something that would be impossible to do except when you consider the political implications. And once you realize that everybody who is currently receiving social security are very active voters and you realize politically this is not what is going to happen. And I'm talking mostly about social security here, but Medicare is the exact same thing.

There is no fund for it. But again, it's just taking money from young poor working people and handing it or using that to pay for the medical expenses of old people who are rich and retired.
