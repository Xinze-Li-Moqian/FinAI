The jobs market is absolutely collapsing with layoff announcements recently exceeding 1.1 million just this year to date, which is the most since 2020. That brings the 2025 year-to-ate total up to 1.17 million. The last time we exceeded 1.1 million was 2020, which is never a year that you want to be compared to. So, a couple of things to note. Number one, these are layoff announcements. That means many of these have not actually happened yet.

These are layoffs that are planned to happen soon or in the near future. So, jobless claims in the US just fell to a three-year low over the Thanksgiving week. Just for clarification, jobless claims is initial jobless claims, and that's people who are filing for [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Unemployment|unemployment]] benefits.

So, this chart that you're looking at right here is the initial jobless claims going back all the way to December of 2021. You can see that since 2021, initial jobless claims numbers are relatively consistent. We had a brief dip below. We had a brief dip below 200 back here in September of 2022.

The second reason is that many of these people who are getting laid off are just finding other jobs.
