# The Fed Just Announced When QT Will End

Recently, Powell was speaking at the National Association for Business Economics in Philadelphia, and he said, "Our long-stated plan is to stop balance sheet runoff when reserves are somewhat above the level we judge consistent with ample reserve conditions." [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Quantitative Tightening (QT)|Quantitative tightening]] [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Quantitative Tightening (QT)|QT]]. This is different than a brokerage account for like you or I where we have to put cash into that account first in order to be able to buy assets.

The [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Federal Reserve|Federal Reserve]] just prints up the money that they need in order to buy any number of assets that they want. When this chart is moving higher, it means they are printing money and buying new assets that they're adding to their balance sheet. When this chart is moving lower, it means the total quantity of assets on their balance sheet is actually shrinking. Now, because the assets that they own are debt, they're either [[Finance/09 Housing and Mortgages/Mortgages|mortgage]] back securities or US [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Treasury Securities and Issuance|treasuries]], it means that sometimes some of these [[Finance/04 Bonds and Funding Markets/Bonds|bonds]] are getting paid off.

like they've got some tea bills and they've got some [[Finance/04 Bonds and Funding Markets/Bonds|bonds]] from the US government. Now, as you see, both in 2018, 2019, and then recently, ever since 2022, the runoff in the balance sheet has been very smooth and consistent. This could not happen if they were just holding assets and then just waiting for some of them to get paid off. and it increases the amount of cash reserves that are available for financial institutions to use and do other things with. The opposite is true as well.
