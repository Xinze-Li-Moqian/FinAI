# Get Your Wealth Out of the System Before it’s Too Late

it might be time to start thinking about getting at least some of your wealth outside the system before it's too late because by the time you need to get your wealth out, you may not be able to. Back in the 1940s, right after World War II ended, the US government's debt to GDP ratio briefly peaked above 120%. Now GDP is the measure of everything produced and sold in an economy in a year. Which means if the United States government implemented a 100% [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Taxation|tax]] on all economic activity for an entire year and use that not to make spend any of the regular spending, but just to pay down the debt, it'd still have 20% left over.

Now this explosion in debt relative to the size of the economy was obviously due to the insane increase in spending to fund the war. And so following this, the government shrank its expenses. The economy grew, had a big increase in [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Productivity|productivity]]. The [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Federal Reserve|Federal Reserve]] engaged in [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Yield Curves|yield curve]] control.

And the government instituted capital controls and [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Financial Repression|financial repression]]. And all of the above forced a growing economy to shoulder the burden of the US government [[Concepts/Finance/Banking and Credit/Deleveraging|deleveraging]] through [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]]. And the government was able to get its own debt relative to the size of the economy down to about 30% after about 40 years. But obviously the story didn't end there because once the government's debt got to a very manageable level relative to the size of the economy, they said, "Well, let's just start borrowing again."

And so starting around 1980, they reversed course and started increasing their leverage yet again, borrowing more and more and more at lower and lower [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Interest and Interest Rates|interest rates]] until we got back up to where we were after World War II. today sitting around 125% debt to GDP ratio which means the government is in the exact same position they were at the end of World War II only this time we don't have a war to end to cancel the spending the spending is on entitlements that are legally required things like social security Medicare which means there's no austerity coming in fact you could cancel the entire discretionary part of the government's budget and they'd still be spending more than they take in [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Taxation|taxes]] so the spending is not slowing down.

The last time we also had basically all of the men come home from war and start working and being productive again. But today all the men are already here working and being productive and in fact most of the women are too which means we do not have a significant increase in the working population right around the corner. So two of the main drivers that helped the government delever last time are not available this time which means they are going to be relying a lot more heavily on the other drivers mainly inflating away the debt.

problem is if you can escape the [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]], then it makes the inflating away the debt part a lot harder for them. They need to have somebody to offload the cost to. So, if you can protect yourself from it, they're not offloading that cost onto you, which makes their job harder. And one of the ways they do this is through [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Financial Repression|financial repression]].

And you can actually go back onto the Fed's own website. This one is richmondfed.org, or and you can read what they say about using [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Financial Repression|financial repression]] as a [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Monetary Policy|monetary policy]] tool, specifically asking the question on whether or not they will come back. These restrictions can include things like domestic [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Interest and Interest Rates|interest rate]] ceilings and capital controls that prevent citizens from buying foreign currencies. And even the Fed admits there is no doubt that they can facilitate deficit financing by creating a captive market for [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Sovereign Debt|government debt]].

That is not a symptom. That is the goal. Now, it is true that back in the 1940s, gold ownership was already illegal. Stock ownership was very difficult. Things like 30-year fixed rate [[Concepts/Finance/Household Finance/Mortgages|mortgages]] on houses didn't exist yet. It is true that the 30-year [[Concepts/Finance/Household Finance/Mortgages|mortgage]] was authorized in 1948, but it wasn't until 1954 that it was extended to existing homes.

And it also wasn't even until the 1960s where it gained widespread acceptance. And even stock ownership was very difficult back then. It was very expensive. You had to have a broker. Commissions were crazy. You had to wait to get paper certificates. [laughter] It was not nearly as easy as it is today to get your cash into assets.

And so [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Financial Repression|financial repression]] was a pretty simple tool for a government to use because a lot of people had most of their savings in savings. And so if you could cap [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Interest and Interest Rates|interest rates]] on what people could get at places like banks, well, they would put their money into US government [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bonds|bonds]]. And if the rate that they were receiving was less than the rate of [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]], they didn't really have any other choice. And discussion of bringing back totalitarian financial controls like this are becoming more popular.

In fact, in just March of this year, the World Economic Forum posted an article about [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Financial Repression|financial repression]] and whether countries should embrace it as their debt load climbs. They specifically use in the wake of World War II as an example of how governments were able to delever. They go on to say that [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Financial Repression|financial repression]] is an attractive prospect to help governments bring down their debt to GDP ratio. In very simple words, what this means in real terms is making it illegal for you to put your wealth into other assets and forcing you to put your wealth or at least some of it into government assets.

In other words, loaning your money to the government. This forces you to destroy your own wealth. You receive interest or growth that is less than the rate of [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]]. Your [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Purchasing Power|purchasing power]] declines and in the process, you are paying off the government's debt for them.

But it's not just the World Economic Forum. Just days ago, President Trump issued a travel ban over golden passports in the Caribbean. One increasingly popular strategy that high- netw worth individuals have begun using is getting second citizenships or at least residencies in other countries to allow them to shift some of their wealth out of the country so that if push comes to shove and the day ever comes where they need to leave, they can. And it looks like the current administration is making moves to make that difficult or maybe even impossible.

So while the opportunity still exists, I think there are five main ways that people can use to get wealth outside the system, whether it is outside the dollar system or outside the country to protect your wealth so that if the day comes where you need it, you already have it because by that time it may be too late to start. And the first I think is probably the most obvious is buying gold. Now, this in particular for this reason that we're discussing today does not include things like GLD or gold mutual funds inside of a [[Concepts/Finance/Household Finance/Retirement Accounts|401k]] or an IRA.

That is not outside the system. The most likely outcome with that is that you do protect your [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Purchasing Power|purchasing power]] as the price of gold rises and the price of those funds rises along with it because they take your money behind the scenes and invest in gold. But if push comes to shove and you need wealth outside the system, you need physical gold waiting for you somewhere. And this means you have it in storage in a different country.

I don't think that it is likely at all that the United States outlaws gold ownership or nationalizes seizes the gold from American citizens again. But if they do, you want some gold at least somewhere else. And there are six vital things that you need to make sure of if you are buying gold in a different country. Number one, you need to make sure that the gold is allocated.

This is kind of like the difference between fractional reserve banking and not allocated gold means you're the only one that owns physical gold there. Unallocated means they have one bar and they have a lot of people who have claims on that one bar, which means if you ever request physical delivery, they'll probably have to go and source that gold and buy it from somewhere else first to be able to deliver it to you. Unallocated gold, I call it goldership. They owe you gold.

Allocated gold is gold ownership. there's an actual piece of gold that you own. Number two, you want that gold segregated. In other words, you have a physical piece that is separated on a shelf in the vault away from somebody else's. There's physical separation.

Number three, you want it insured so that if there is any theft or any loss for any reason that the company and your gold there is insured. Number four, you want it independently audited. There are plenty of companies that show their audits, but they're doing their audits themselves. You want independent audits of the vaults.

Number five, you want these vaults to be private, not government controlled. It is too easy for governments to nationalize and take over. In other words, steal gold if it is already in a vault that belongs to the government. So, you want your gold stored in a private company vault.

And then number six, you want no history of wealth confiscation, specifically gold confiscation. And that's why, I mean, we're talking about getting wealth outside the US system. So, you're also not going to want it in the country that you live in. But the United States is a country with a rich history of gold theft.

FDR outlawed gold ownership and stole it from all American citizens. Then Nixon stole gold from the rest of the world. Canada has history with at least temporary wealth confiscation. In other words, taking groups of people and saying, "No, you cannot have access to the money or the wealth that is yours."

The Bank of England has history with wealth confiscation, specifically gold confiscation, telling other countries, "No, you cannot have the gold." So you want your vault located in a country that has a high degree of economic freedom, really good rule of law, and has no history of wealth confiscation by the government. If you do this, you'll be ahead of 90 95% of people because if push comes to shove someday, and you do have to leave, the government starts taking your wealth, [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Financial Repression|financial repression]], capital controls. You already have wealth outside the system that they cannot access, that they do not know about.

Number two, this is going to be controversial because I know a lot of you do not like [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]], but [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]] is permissionless. If you hold your [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]] in cold storage, you can go anywhere in the world with a phrase in your head and take that wealth with you anywhere. You can use it for payments without permission. It's what peer-to-peer means.

There's no big brother watching over your shoulder telling you, "No, you can't send that wire or why do you need to get this cash?" Having at least some of your wealth in [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]] is a hedge against a totalitarian financial regime. Number three is real estate. And this is true in a couple of different ways.

The first way is just your regular standard. Get a single family home with a renter in it. Make sure that property cash flows so that it's paying for its own debt service. You're not negative every month. And you have a 30-year fixed rate [[Concepts/Finance/Household Finance/Mortgages|mortgage]] on it.

The reason for this is because in today's day and age, that 30-year fixed rate [[Concepts/Finance/Household Finance/Mortgages|mortgage]] is really the asset. The house is just the collateral that allows you to have the asset. The reason why getting your wealth outside the system means in part having a 30-year fixed rate [[Concepts/Finance/Household Finance/Mortgages|mortgage]] is because technically that is a short position on the dollar. If we expect a possibility of [[Concepts/Finance/Banking and Credit/Deleveraging|deleveraging]] through [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]], [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Yield Curves|yield curve]] control, [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Financial Repression|financial repression]], capital controls, that means the [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Purchasing Power|purchasing power]] of the dollar goes down.

You want the ability to [[Concepts/Economics/Microeconomics/Profit|profit]] from that. Technically speaking, a short position means you borrow something and then exchange it for something else. So when you borrow dollars and exchange it for real estate, you owe back dollars.

When you put a tenant in place, they pay you back the dollars that you owe for you. You don't have to come up with that yourself from your own earned income. that allows you to [[Concepts/Economics/Microeconomics/Profit|profit]] from a decrease in the [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Purchasing Power|purchasing power]] of the dollar, giving you a hedge against future [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Financial Repression|financial repression]]. Now, the other way you can use real estate as getting your wealth outside the system is by buying real estate in other countries. There are other countries, plenty of them actually, that allow paths to residency or even citizenship through purchasing real estate in those countries. The fourth way to get wealth outside the system is very unconventional when you're talking about getting your wealth outside the system, but it's starting a business. Even if it's a side hustle, just something small that you're doing in addition to your regular 9 to5.

The reason for this is because the skill to make money is an asset that nobody can ever take from you. There's a reason why for the last hundred years, 200 years, as immigrants have moved to the United States, whether from Ireland or Italy or anywhere, many times they are entrepreneurs. They're small business owners. The knowledge and the skills to be able to work for yourself and make money without being employed by a large corporation allow you to go anywhere in the world and take those skills with you and still know how to make money.
