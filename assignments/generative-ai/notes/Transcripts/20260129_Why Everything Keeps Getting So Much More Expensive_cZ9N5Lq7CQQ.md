# Why Everything Keeps Getting So Much More Expensive

Well, the data is in. For most of us and for the vast majority of things that people care about buying, life is getting a lot more expensive. And there is a common denominator that is causing these things to get more expensive. Take a look at this chart.

Prices from 2000 through 2025 for most of the things that make the biggest impact on people's lives have continued to get more and more expensive. You can see at the very top of this list we have hospital services which have gotten 281% more expensive. We have college tuition and fees, college textbooks, child care, medical care services, housing and food and beverages all getting wildly more expensive. Now you might see that average hourly [[Finance/11 Employment and the Real Economy/Wages|wages]] have also gone up a good amount during that time.

But keep in mind that's average and averages are skewed by the top end. This is not median. So median income is going to look a little bit worse. However, as we scroll down this list, you can see there are some things that haven't really gotten much more expensive, like household furniture and clothing.

And there are some other things that have actually gotten cheaper over that time. Things like cell phones, computer software, and of course, TVs. Now, I tweeted this picture out or posted it on X, saying that I thought whoever was in charge of TV prices should also be put in charge of prices of healthcare, education, and housing. Obviously being facitious here because nobody is in charge of the price of TVs and that's the point.

People are in charge of the price of housing, hospital services, medical care, and education. And I know at least in the United States, they're not outright directly controlled with the prices being set by a central planner, but they have absolutely by far the most intervention from the central planners. And that brings me to the first common denominator to what causes these things to get more expensive, which is regulations telling people what they can and cannot do. In a completely free market, producers are able and free to produce whatever they want, and then they go try to get consumers to voluntarily purchase the things that they've produced.

If consumers feel like they want that thing more than they want the money that they are giving up, they will purchase that thing and they will be better off because they will get something they want more by giving up something they want less. That's how free market exchange works. And both parties are better off as a result, otherwise known as [[Finance/16 Economic Reasoning/Profit|profit]]. And because of this [[Finance/16 Economic Reasoning/Profit|profit]] opportunity, it attracts more producers to try and make better goods for even cheaper so they can get the business instead.

This process, which is a free market process, drives quality up over time and prices down over time. No intervention needed. But in all of the areas where the government comes in and says, "Nope, if you're going to make this thing, you have to make it this way. You can't buy this thing from that person.

You have to buy it from a person that we have approved. You can't make that thing with those ingredients. You have to make it with these ingredients. You can't do it with this many people. You have to do it with this many people."

or any of the millions of ways the government intervenes in business, it drives prices up. And to the extent that it drives prices up, it means that competition goes down, which means quality suffers and so do prices. Now, the second similarity that you will notice between all things that get the most expensive is government-g granted monopolies. When a third party like the government who exercises a monopoly on violence steps in and says, "Nobody is allowed to do this unless we say they are allowed to do this."

That's a government-g granted monopoly. There's a reason your internet service provider sucks. It's because the government outlaws competition. And it's only recently because we've finally gotten technology to a point where we don't need the cables or the fiber in the ground to get internet that we finally have some competition coming from people who are delivering that internet from space and are now forcing the traditional internet service providers to step up their game a little bit.

Government sanctioned monopolies again protect the incumbents driving down quality driving up price. This is another reason why medication is so expensive because governments grant monopolies to whoever says they had the idea first or whoever purchases the ability to say I had the idea first. Most medical research is not done by the big pharma companies. It's done by universities who are by the way funded by taxpayers.

They're doing the research for free because we are footing the bill. And then guess who comes in and scoops up the patents? Big Pharma. The current GLP-1 or Ozmpic medications are a great example of this because they are extremely easy to manufacture. They are so easy to manufacture in fact that if you get these things through the black market from people who do not care about the law, you can get these medications for literally dollars per month.

Whereas if you buy the official ones from Novo Nordis or Eli Liy, you can pay hundreds or even thousands of times more than that because they have the Monopoly on violence getting their back and saying, "No, these are the only people allowed to sell this thing cuz they had the idea first or they purchased the idea first." And without those artificial guard rails set up around free market exchange, pretty much all medication would have the same exact effect on their pricing as TVs do. Better quality, lower prices. But this is not the only thing driving up prices.

Obviously, this is a chart that I really hope you're familiar with, unless you're new to the channel, which if you are, welcome. I'm Joe Brown and I make these videos because I try to make money simple so that you can make more money. More simply, this is a chart of M2, which is the money supply. In other words, it is all of the dollars in circulation in things like checking accounts, savings accounts, and brokerage accounts.

And as you can see, for the most part, this chart only goes up and to the right. There was a brief period of time when the money supply was shrinking, when the Fed was trying to undo their mistakes from all the money printing in 2020 and 2021. But as you can see, it really didn't undo that much of the money printing and it is well on its way past that old all-time high and continuing to increase even today. The reason why money printing causes prices to go up is because all else being equal, it means you have more money chasing the same amount of goods and services.

Imagine if we push this to extreme so that we can understand what's going on here. Imagine every single person in their bank account got deposited a million dollars. Suddenly, everyone would feel like they were a million dollars richer, but they would soon find out they would go and try and spend that money and they wouldn't be able to buy things at the same prices they used to be able to. Because, for example, if I had a million dollars dropped into my bank account, I may not be willing to go sell my time at Taco Bell or McDonald's for $12 an hour.

I've got a million bucks in the bank account, so I'm going to charge more for my labor now because I don't want to give up my time for such a small amount. I don't need to anymore. So [[Finance/11 Employment and the Real Economy/Wages|wages]] would instantly skyrocket. So the prices of products.

I would go to the store to buy something and suddenly if there was any supply left because maybe I'm not the first one there and everybody's already purchased everything off the shelves. If there is something left, the store owner may not be willing to sell me that toilet paper or that milk or those eggs for just a couple dollars anymore cuz again he just got a million bucks dropped in his bank account. So he'd rather hold on to those things now unless he's able to get a sufficiently high price. Now obviously when you look at this chart as the money supply goes up it's not being done in a vacuum.

In other words I said earlier that all else being equal it's more money chasing the same amount of goods and services. That's not actually what happens in reality because we do make more goods and services. That's what progress is. That's what growth is.

That's what an increase of wealth is. We're constantly figuring out ways to make clothing and food and transportation more efficient and cheaper. This is the effect of the free market driving up quality driving down prices. So if the money supply always stayed constant and never changed, prices of everything would actually get cheaper over time.

And this is what has happened throughout all of human history outside of the brief few decades at a time where governments intervene drive the money supply up and therefore prices go up as well. In the world we live in today, though, neither the money supply nor the supply of wealth stays exactly the same. They're both growing sometimes at about the same rate. And in fact, that's the [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Federal Reserve|Federal Reserve's]] actual job.

You can take a look at the [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Federal Reserve|Federal Reserve]] Reform Act of 1977, which is the act of Congress that gave the [[Finance/01 Central Banking/Federal Reserve|Federal Reserve]] their current mandate. You can see here in the first sentence, it says that they shall maintain long-run growth of the monetary and credit aggregates commensurate with the economy's long-run potential to increase production. In plain English, that means increase the money supply along with the growth in the economy. And just to make sure that the growth of wealth doesn't outpace the growth of the money supply because god forbid prices get a little bit cheaper for everybody, they always make sure the money supply grows a little bit faster so that prices grow a little bit at a time.

And of course, sometimes they wildly overshoot and prices shoot up dramatically. So even without all the government intervention and the laws and regulations and the government granted monopolies that artificially make some things way more expensive, you would still have a rise in prices of approximately everything simply because they continue to increase the money supply which decreases the [[Finance/02 Inflation and Purchasing Power/Purchasing Power|purchasing power]] of money along the way. But we can take this one step further because it's not even just the cost of individual items. Obviously we can see that there are certain things that have gotten wildly more expensive over time and some things that have gotten cheaper.

In reality, when something gets cheaper, it means you can afford more wealth. A couple thousand years ago, most people, even kings, couldn't really afford that much wealth. Doesn't matter how many horses or men you have at your command, you couldn't get around the globe in 12 hours a couple thousand years ago, even a couple hundred years ago. Doesn't matter how many men and horses and kingdoms you have at your disposal.

You are not going to be able to get even the basic necessities that we have today like air conditioning and running hot water, modern-day hygiene, simple pain reliever medications. There's so much wealth that we have at our disposal that is so cheap today. That is the definition of wealth. With the output that you have of your time and your labor and your skills, you can afford to get the things you need and want.

That process of wealth growing and people having access to be able to afford those things more and more cheaply given their outputs is a product of the free market. It's a product of innovation. It's a product of invention. It's a product of progress.

And that happens more and more when you have more and more freedom. The more governments get involved, the less wealthy people are. And in fact, you can see this. Again, it's not just individual items. This happens for societies and nations at large. The more economic freedom something has, the more wealth, [[Finance/02 Inflation and Purchasing Power/Purchasing Power|purchasing power]] or income it has.

You can see countries like Zimbabwe, Iran, Egypt, China, Argentina have less economic freedom and therefore their income per capita is much much lower. Whereas countries like Singapore, Switzerland, Hong Kong, Canada, Denmark, Norway, Ireland, Luxembourg, New Zealand have much more economic freedom and therefore much more income per capita. The more freedom people have, the more income, more [[Finance/02 Inflation and Purchasing Power/Purchasing Power|purchasing power]], the more they can afford wealth. And as a final note, it doesn't matter who is in power.

It doesn't matter what beliefs the person has who is in power. It doesn't matter whether it's the right or the left. Doesn't matter the name of the party. And it doesn't matter what they are using the power of the government for. If they're increasing the power of the government, if they're increasing regulations, if they're increasing [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Government Spending|government spending]], if they're increasing government borrowing, if they're increasing [[Finance/03 Government Debt and Fiscal Policy/Taxation|taxation]], if they're increasing their amount of control on the economy, the result is less freedom, not more.

And if you are a rational person and you want more access to more wealth, you want things like education and health care and food and housing and luxuries to be cheaper and more attainable for everybody, then you want more freedom. You want less government. You want more liberty. And unfortunately, everybody who disagrees is either a religious ideologue to their political belief system or they're just unaware of the data.
