# How Gold Could Become Money Again

Is it possible that the world could actually ever return to a [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Gold Standard|gold standard]]? And I don't mean a gold exchange standard. I mean gold is used as money. Most people in today's day and age dismiss that as impossible because we live in a global world with the internet with trillions of dollars being transacted across country borders by the day.

And while it may be improbable given some of the problems with a [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Gold Standard|gold standard]] that I will go over in a moment, anything is possible. I mean, just consider if you had asked somebody a hundred years ago if it was possible that at some point in the future, everybody would be using fake magic internet money that the government could create out of thin air and people would just use it and go along with their lives. Most people probably would have thought that sounded preposterous. Yet, here we are.

If the world is to use [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Gold as a Monetary Asset|gold as money]] again, it will likely not be the way it was used originally. For thousands of years, when gold was used as money, there were physical coins that were being transacted. In other words, anytime you bought or sold anything, there was physical metal that was physically moving from one location to another. Because what matters when you are transacting with gold is moving the ownership of that gold from one person to another.

If I'm going to move the ownership of a sandwich from you to me, I need to move the ownership of the gold for payment from me to you. And for thousands of years, this was the way it was done. This meant that transacting across large distances was very costly because you had to load the gold up onto camel's backs or onto ships and you had to pay for security to make sure it didn't get stolen. You have to pay for all of the food, all the fuel, and all the supplies necessary to move just the payment.

And then you also have to consider the cost of moving the actual goods. Another problem is the problem of [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Deflation|deflation]]. Now, [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Deflation|deflation]] is a good thing. It's a natural thing. As the world creates more wealth, assuming the money supply stays relatively constant, then the price of all goods and services go down over time.

This is a good thing because it means your savings slowly grow in value over time. It means your [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Wages|wages]] slowly grow in value over time. But the problem is that whatever you are using as money eventually gets too valuable for small transactions. This is why other less valuable metals were used as change for gold.

[[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Silver|Silver]] coins, copper coins, they were used as change because it just wasn't practical to slice up gold into small enough denominations for everyday transactions. And then sometime around the 1600s, a new monetary invention started to make gold much more usable and fixed some of those prior problems that we just discussed. This was the invention of paper. And I know this eventually led to the downfall of gold being used as money.

But stay with me here because the original intent and reasons why paper was used actually solved a bunch of problems. Because like I mentioned earlier, it doesn't matter if the gold actually moves if you can just transfer the ownership of that gold. And so what people started doing was leaving their gold in banks or with trusted third parties or with goldsmiths and they would get paper receipts or notes or bills that said that if they brought that back in, they could get a specific weight. And so instead of me having to redeem my paper for gold to make a payment and then that new person go put the gold back in storage, the paper started trading as a proxy for the gold.

So the gold's ownership was changing hands anytime payments were being made, but the gold didn't actually have to move. This meant that gold became far cheaper to use as money, especially across large distances. physical gold would only move from one vault to another for large transactions where it actually made sense to pay for that massive movement or batch settlement between vaults between banks. But for pretty much everything else, people were comfortable just trading the ownership of the gold and leaving the gold where it was.

This led to faster transactions, cheaper payments, and because the paper could represent any weight of gold, it meant gold could be used for smaller transactions now, which began the demonetization of [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Silver|silver]]. Most people are not aware of this, but [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Silver|silver's]] demonetization process started hundreds of years ago with the invention of paper. Because if you put 1 ounce of gold on deposit with a banker, you could get 100 pieces of paper to represent that 1 oz of gold. you could get a thousand pieces of paper to represent that 1 ounce of gold.

And so paper made gold infinitely divisible, essentially free to use as payment and faster to transact across large distances, which helped increase globalization and brought a huge boom of production and increase in global wealth. Now obviously most of you are aware of the problem that was introduced with this mainly that that led to centralization. It meant all the gold got sucked out of circulation because people didn't need to use it for payments anymore and it just wound up sitting in all the vaults. Eventually, this led to the practice of fraud, which was fractional reserve banking, where the banks who were holding on to the gold would create more papers that could be redeemed for gold than they actually had gold.

This caused local booms and busts as there was the expansion and contraction of credit, [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]] and [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Deflation|deflation]], caused [[Concepts/Finance/Banking and Credit/Bank Runs|bank runs]], and all sorts of problems. But instead of outlawing this practice, nations wanted to do it themselves. And so they just nationalized banks and said, "Okay, every bank can do this, but they have to be underneath the [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Central Banks|central bank]] so that we can do it the most." And so this centralization of gold led to the creation of fractional reserve banking, booms and busts, panics, [[Concepts/Finance/Banking and Credit/Bank Runs|bank runs]], [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]] and [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Deflation|deflation]], [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Recessions|recessions]] and depressions, [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Central Banks|central banks]], infinite funding for governments, and many more problems.

Eventually, these largecale national defaults from nationalized fractional reserve banking, confiscating the gold from citizens and then just defaulting on the obligation to redeem the paper for gold meant that the world was plunged into using government paper as money without it being redeemable for anything like gold anymore. Obviously, today that has transitioned into digital paper. Now, there are two main functions that money serves in an economy. One is a store of value and two is medium of exchange.

These are fairly self-explanatory, but for a medium of exchange, it means that's the thing that is typically used on the other side of any transaction. So, when you sell your labor or your time to an employer, they give you money, the medium of exchange. When you want to buy a cheeseburger or a car, you give the other person money, the medium of exchange. Now the other function was store of value which just means that that is where you store the value of your labor or your product or your service until you are ready to exchange it for something that is real real wealth, real goods, real services, an asset, something you want to consume.

It's just the holding place. It is not the wealth itself. It's where you store the value of your wealth until you're ready to go get actual wealth. Now nine times out of 10, store of values and mediums of exchange are going to be the same thing.

But whenever there is a monetary transition from one form of money to another, if it is organic and not forced by the government, typically what happens is the former form of money will accelerate in its medium of exchangess and it will decelerate in its store of valuess. A new form of money that is going to displace it will come in and first serve as more of a store of value and then only later will it transition to also being the medium of exchange. Now, if that makes no sense, what I'm describing here is [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Greshams Law|Gresham's law]] that says bad money drives out good.

If you think about gold or many people think about [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]] this way or maybe other currencies today, you as a selfpreserving person who wants to make sure that you benefit yourself are rationally going to want to stock up on the thing that you think is going to be more valuable in the future, gold, [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]], something else. you are not going to want to voluntarily use that for payment. You're going to want to get rid of the other things that you have that you think will be less valuable in the future.

In this case, dollars. So, you want to spend your dollars as much as you possibly can while you hoard gold or [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]] or something else as much as you possibly can. We see this in the data happening today. The [[Concepts/Finance/International Finance/US Dollar in Global Finance|US dollar's]] share of international transactions has been growing and is now over 50% of all Swift traffic.

So the dollar is being used more and more as a medium of exchange being used for payments. However, you can also see that [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Treasury Securities and Issuance|treasuries]] are on the decline as a percent of foreign reserves while gold is on the rise as a percent of foreign reserves. A lot of this obviously has to do with the price changing. I mean gold has doubled in a relatively short amount of time recently.

But [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Central Banks|central banks]] are accumulating more and more gold. And so at least partially we are seeing this [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Greshams Law|Gresham's law]] play out on the global stage where dollars are being used for payment more and more. So that medium of exchangess is increasing but the store of valuess of dollars is going down as people and institutions and countries and nations and sovereign wealth funds and [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Central Banks|central banks]] dump them for other alternative forms of money that they want to hold on to longer like gold. And this process will continue as long as people can make it continue until sellers no longer are willing to accept the old form of money as payment anymore.

Right now, I'm assuming you're still willing to sell your labor or your time or your product or your service in exchange for dollars. Assuming the dollar price is high enough, you are still willing to accept it because it's still money. I know some of you say, "Oh, it's currency. It's not money."

Semantics. Leave your 1900's dictionaries in the past. We're using modern parlance here. If the vast majority of the world is willing to accept it as payment, then it's still money. But there may come a time, probably not soon, when that is no longer the case.

When you might be willing to accept gold or [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|bitcoin]] as payment, but there are no number, there's no amount, there's no quantity of dollars, whether it's a million or a billion or a trillion dollars, you'd be willing to accept for payment for that same thing. Now that might sound preposterous in the current day and age that we live in, but consider this $100 trillion bill from Zimbabwe. This is what happens when people no longer are willing to accept a money as payment. Now, I mentioned that [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Central Banks|central banks]] are accumulating gold right now.

That is one of the factors that has caused gold to go up to $4,000 an ounce recently. But what most people are not aware of is that the vast majority of the time physical gold is still not moving. The same thing is done today as has been done for hundreds of years. There are trusted third parties who hold the gold in their vaults and only the ownership of that gold is changing hands.

Gold will leave the vaults or most of the time it just moves from one vault to another. when there is batch settlement. Sometimes there's repatriation when a country says, "Hey, like Poland said, hey, we're going to move all of our gold from England and we're going to take it back here." And then sometimes institutions stand for delivery on contracts.

But again, it's not that common. The vast majority of gold ownership, changing hands, is done without the gold moving. Think of like GLD. When institutions like large hedge funds like Bridgewwater, Ray Dalio's hedge fund, when they buy and sell gold, they're doing that with shares of GLD, an ETF, all that gold is still staying in the vault.

When sovereign wealth funds are getting exposure to gold through vaulting, or many people are buying unallocated gold, it's all staying put. Now, the big problem here still exists that always existed, is that you have to be able to trust and verify that the gold is actually there. Now, here's the hard truth for all of the gold bugs out there who are buying those gold backs, which is that looks like a dollar bill, but it's actually laminated with gold. People who are storing up on gold and [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Silver|silver]] coins, unless we have a nuclear apocalypse and all technology gets wiped out.

We will never go back to a barter system where gold and [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Silver|silver]] coins are being used as money, where they actually have to move. It just cannot happen. All [[Concepts/Economics/International Economics/International Trade|global trade]] would instantly collapse. That is not a world that anybody wants to live in.

But just like has been done for hundreds of years, it is possible in the future that gold ownership changing hands electronically with the gold staying put is how gold could be used as money again. Just like it's still being done today when gold is traded at scale from [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Central Banks|central banks]] and large institutions. And this can work as long as delivery is available. Now, whether we're talking about some hypothetical future or you're looking at buying gold today and you're not able to hold on to it yourself, here are the things that must be in place in order for you to be able to trust through verification that the gold is there as much as possible.

Number one, you need to make sure that the gold is allocated. Unallocated means that they have some gold and they are selling you a claim on some of that gold, but it's like fractional reserve. It's like goldership, not gold ownership. They sell you a claim on gold and they just say, "We're going to owe you some gold."

Which means if you and everybody else come to try and get your gold from them, they have to go source it from the open market. They don't actually have enough there allocated to each person. So you want to make sure the gold is allocated. Number two, you want to make sure it is segregated.

That means your physical gold is actually separately held. It's a physical piece. Sometimes it's serialized, sometimes it's an actual coin, but you're not just taking a percentage of a pool. There's actual physical molecules that belong to you. Number three, you want to make sure it is insured.

Now, most of these are obviously going to be insured, but still good to check on anyway. That way, if there is ever some sort of theft or loss or anything like that, they have some big insurance in order to cover the losses. Number four, you want to make sure it is independently audited. And I mean independently audited by a separate company.

You don't want them doing their own audits like the US government does at Fort Knox. You want to make sure there is an uninterested, unbiased third party coming in and making sure that the gold they say is there is actually there. Number five, you ideally want the gold to be stored in a country with a high degree of economic freedom. The easiest way to find this is to go to heritage.org index of economic freedom and you can sort by overall score and you can see the countries that have the highest degrees of economic freedom every single year.

They rank countries on things like property rights, government integrity, judicial effectiveness, [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Taxation|tax]] burden, and [[Concepts/Economics/Public Economics/Government Spending|government spending]]. Although for our purposes, those last two are really irrelevant. And then finally, and this is something that most people skip over, you want to make sure that that country has no history of gold confiscation. You want to make sure that there's no legal precedent for that country to go in and say, "Okay, all the gold that's in all the banks or all the vaults, we own that now."

And guess what? America has done that twice. First in 1934 when we confiscated all the gold from all American citizens. And then second in 1971 when America confiscated all gold from the entire world. Now, I say confiscated, I don't mean they went out and grabbed it all immediately after that.

I mean they were the ones storing it all and then they just said, "Nope, now it's ours." And so for a gold monetary system to work, or if you want to just store your gold somewhere to work, you need all those things in place, allocated, segregated, insured, independently audited in a country with high degree of economic freedom and no history of confiscation. And if you think governments will never allow this to happen, you are right. They would never voluntarily allow this to happen.

But every time throughout history, whenever there is a change of money from a government fiat back to gold, which has happened countless times, it's because people no longer accept the government's money as payment. It's the only way money's ever change. And once people no longer accept the government's money as payment, the government has no choice but to switch over to trying to get the money that people will accept as payment. Because if they can't pay anybody to enforce any of their rules, they have no power.

And so governments will be forced to use the money that people are willing to accept. It's impossible to know the future. We don't know whether gold will become money again in the future, whether [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]] will become money because it does solve many of those problems that I talked about about trust and verification and speed and efficiency, but it also has its own set of problems. And we don't know how much longer the dollar will reign supreme.

Which is why I advocate spreading your bets around a little bit. Own some [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]], own some gold. You still obviously have to have some dollars and obviously you want to maintain a portfolio of assets that are also hedges against [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]] like real estate and [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Stocks|stocks]], but you don't have to go allin on something like gold or [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]]. And don't listen to anybody who says you do.

And if gold ever does become money again, it will be used the exact same way that you use dollars today. You will swipe a debit card. You will swipe a credit card. Prices will be denominated in ounces or grams of gold instead of dollars.

And the ownership of the gold that you are spending will move from you to somebody else and the gold will remain in the vault. And when and if vaults need to, they will do batch settlement between each other moving some of the gold to settle those larger transactions just like institutions and [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Central Banks|central banks]] already do today.
