# Why Gold is Rising So Much, and So Quickly

Gold is on the move again. And the big question is why? And the answer is nobody knows. It is after all just a worthless shiny rock, right? Wrong. Gold is moving up for a number of reasons. First, let's take a look at the price.

Gold is now above $3,500 per ounce after breaking out from a multimonth consolidation pattern where it tested resistance at 3430 five separate times since April of 2025. Now, $3,500 per ounce is significant because it is 1,000 times more than what the price of gold was when we delinct from the [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Gold Standard|gold standard]] back in 1971. For decades, the price of gold had been held at $35 per ounce by law, meaning that every $35 were redeemable by certain parties for an ounce of gold. And when the United States had issued too many dollars to make good on that promise, we told the rest of the world, "Sorry, you can't actually have your gold."

And from that point, the price of gold has consistently, though with lots of volatility, moved higher and higher to the point where today it is 1,000 times higher than where it was a little over 50 years ago. So the first reason gold is moving which is very important to know when you are investing in gold is that gold tends to anticipate the effects of [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Monetary Policy|monetary policy]]. Here's what I mean by that. If you take a look at this chart, this is the price of gold from 2014 through 2020.

And you can see that the price of gold for years was volatile but moved sideways, never being able to jump above that 1346 price. But starting in June of 2019, the price of gold broke out and started to move higher. Now, you may not remember this because it was about 6 years ago now. But mid to late 2019 was when banks started to run into problems.

Liquidity in the financial system was drying up. And in September of 2019 was when the Fed had to start not [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Quantitative Easing (QE)|QE]] in order to bail out the [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Repurchase Agreements (Repo)|repo market]]. Gold began its move right before that and continued its move higher through the money printing that started in 2020. Ultimately rising 50% before it topped out in August of 2020.

Keep in mind by August of 2020 zero [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]] from the money printing had hit the economy yet. Prices of goods and services had not started moving. And yet the price of gold peaked and started to move down from there, ultimately moving sideways again for a couple of years. Now again, this is because gold anticipates the effects of [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Monetary Policy|monetary policy]].

By the time [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]] came around from all that money printing, the market was looking forward at the tightening the Fed was going to have to do. But gold had already made its move by the time the [[Concepts/Economics/Macroeconomics/Inflation|inflation]] came around. And we see a very similar pattern playing out right now from 2020 all the way through 2024. Price of gold moved sideways and tested resistance around $2,000 an ounce multiple times.

And when it finally broke out starting in March of 2024, the move was anticipating the future results of [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Monetary Policy|monetary policy]] changing [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Interest and Interest Rates|interest rate]] cuts and future [[Concepts/Finance/Central Banking/Quantitative Easing (QE)|quantitative easing]] in order to deal with the government's bloated spending and borrowing. This is [[Concepts/Economics/Monetary Economics/Greshams Law|Gresham's law]] in action. Bad money drives good money out of circulation. In other words, people will hoard the forms of money that store their value the best and they won't get rid of it.

They won't sell it and they will get rid of the forms of money that don't hold their value. This is why you hold gold and you use dollars for payments. This is why you hold [[Concepts/Finance/Markets and Instruments/Bitcoin|Bitcoin]] and use dollars for transactions. Only at that time does a currency move from a medium of exchange and be replaced by something that has been serving as a store of value. In other words, a money's store of value function will be replaced by a superior form of store of value. In other words, between a money's two functions of being a store of value and a medium of exchange, the store of value function will be replaced first by a alternate store of value.
