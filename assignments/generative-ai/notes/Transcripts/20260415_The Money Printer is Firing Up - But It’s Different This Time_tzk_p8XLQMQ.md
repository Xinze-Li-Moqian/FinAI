The problem with this is that money isn't actually printed into existence. It's loaned into existence. So every dollar that adds to the total supply of dollars at the same exact time adds to the future demand for dollars. In other words, the total debt load always has to increase faster than the total money supply growth.

This is not a huge problem if the money supply growth goes to fund new production and economic growth. So on one end of the extreme, we have evenly distributed money printing that has essentially no effect on real wealth.

On the other end of the extreme, you have perfectly isolated money printing that has no effect on wealth. But what about somewhere in the middle? What if they do some money printing and then hand it out to some people? Well, the first people who get their hands on that new money get to spend it at prices that have not yet changed.
