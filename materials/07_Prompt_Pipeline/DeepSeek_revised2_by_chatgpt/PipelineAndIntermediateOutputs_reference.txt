1. Per-speech Phase 1 Structured Record

Each source speech has one JSON record containing:

– filename
– date
– local central claim cc
– local themes t
– local argument conclusions
– premises
– evidence
– analogies and rhetorical devices
– unverifiable claims
– source excerpts / locations where available
– Claims Inventory fields

The local central claim and local themes remain source-level entities. They
are not overwritten by canonical labels.


2. Per-speech Phase 4 Remapped Record

Phase 4 creates one lossless augmented record per speech.

The remapped record preserves the complete Phase 1 content unchanged and adds:

– primary canonical CC ID, or uncategorised status
– primary_cc_relationship_type
– primary_cc_relationship_explanation
– primary canonical CC confidence
– classification prompt and schema versions
– canonical Theme IDs
– local-to-canonical mapping records
– confidence or similarity values
– mapping rationale
– Canonical CC × T pair IDs
– default bucket memberships
– unmapped or ambiguous flags
– optional secondary CC contributions, only when explicitly enabled
– canonical model/version identifier

For every mapped local argument, Phase 4 also creates a source-contribution
record containing at least:

– contribution_id
– speech_id, filename, date, and source_hash
– pair_id, cc_id, and theme_id
– membership_type
– primary_cc_relationship_type
– primary_cc_relationship_explanation
– primary_cc_confidence
– classification_prompt_version and classification_schema_version
– local_theme_id and local_theme_text
– Theme-mapping confidence and rationale
– local_argument_id and conclusion
– preserved premises, evidence, and analogies

The source contribution must inherit the speech's accepted relationship type,
explanation, confidence, and classification versions exactly. Python validates
this propagation; the Phase 4 LLM is not permitted to reinterpret the role.

Phase 4 is an augmentation and mapping operation, not a semantic rewrite.
An optional Markdown view may be rendered from the structured record, but the
structured remapped record remains authoritative.

Remapped record (role definition, moved from the ER document's Roles list)
A lossless Phase 4 augmentation of the Phase 1 speech record. It adds
canonical mappings and pair memberships without replacing or paraphrasing the
local intellectual content.


3. Canonicalisation and Assembly Pipeline Flow

Local records
→ canonical CC discovery
→ canonical Theme discovery
→ local-to-canonical mappings
→ persistent CC × T pair records
→ runtime CC × T buckets
→ canonical argument synthesis
→ authoritative compendium sections
→ Theme Cross-Index and complete theme_index.md

The system must preserve the distinction between:

1. source speech entities;
2. canonical entities;
3. persistent relationship entities;
4. temporary runtime aggregation containers; and
5. rendered publication text.

[Note: item 3 above ("persistent relationship entities") and item 4
("temporary runtime aggregation containers") are, concretely, the Canonical
CC × T pair and the runtime CC × T bucket — defined in the ER document's
Section 2, not here, since they are part of the ER model rather than pipeline
mechanics. This taxonomy is retained here because it is itself a statement
about pipeline architecture — which category of entity a given pipeline stage
is allowed to produce or consume — rather than a definition of the entities
themselves.]


4. Phase 5 Relationship-Aware Bucket Organisation and Synthesis — Process Behavior

For each persistent Canonical CC × T pair, Python instantiates one runtime
bucket and groups its validated source contributions by the controlled
speech-to-chapter relationship type before any synthesis call.

The bucket input contains:

– pair metadata fixed by Python;
– relationship_type_counts;
– source_contributions_by_relationship_type;
– validated source IDs, filenames, excerpts, and local argument content.

The LLM synthesises only inside the fixed pair and must respect the stored
roles:

– supports, specific_instance, mechanism, consequence, application, and
  evidence contributions may develop the affirmative case;
– qualification contributions must remain limits, conditions, refinements, or
  exceptions;
– opposition contributions must remain explicit challenges, disagreements, or
  contrary evidence;
– historical_example contributions must be described as illustrations or
  precedents and must not automatically be treated as proof.

The LLM must not convert qualification or opposition into support, invent a
consensus, change a relationship type, add source records, or move content to a
different CC × T pair. Python validates the returned source-contribution IDs,
derives canonical-argument relationship_roles from the accepted contributions,
and ensures that qualification and opposition are represented in
Disagreements and Qualifications where relevant.

[Note: the bucket's structural definition — what a runtime bucket is, what it
contains, and its relationship to the persistent pair — is in the ER
document's Section 2, Junction Tables and Runtime Buckets, and is not repeated
here. Only the process behavior governing what the LLM may and may not do
during synthesis is retained in this document.]
