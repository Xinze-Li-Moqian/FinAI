Notation
--------
Canonical = normalised and deduplicated across the corpus.

CC = canonical Central Claim
T  = canonical Theme
cc = per-speech/local central claim
t  = per-speech/local theme

A Canonical Central Claim is a full proposition with a verb.
A Canonical Theme is a noun phrase with no verb.
A Canonical CC × T pair is the persistent junction record connecting one
Canonical Central Claim to one Canonical Theme.
A runtime CC × T bucket is the temporary aggregation container created from
that persistent pair during Phase 5 assembly. A bucket is not text and is not
contained inside a speech.

Speech-to-Canonical-CC relationship = the explicit argumentative role that a
categorised speech plays relative to its one primary Canonical Central Claim
chapter. Chapter assignment is based on the best argumentative home for the
speech, not on a requirement that the local central claim be a paraphrase of,
or semantically equivalent to, the complete canonical proposition.

Every categorised speech must have exactly one controlled primary relationship
type and a non-empty relationship explanation. The controlled vocabulary is:

– supports
  The speech directly argues that the Canonical Central Claim is true.

– specific_instance
  The speech presents a narrower, concrete, or more specific instance of the
  broader Canonical Central Claim.

– mechanism
  The speech explains a process through which the Canonical Central Claim
  operates or could become true.

– consequence
  The speech explains a result, implication, or downstream effect of the
  Canonical Central Claim.

– qualification
  The speech limits, conditions, refines, or adds an exception to the
  Canonical Central Claim.

– opposition
  The speech challenges, disputes, or presents contrary evidence against the
  Canonical Central Claim.

– historical_example
  The speech supplies a historical illustration, analogy, or precedent that
  bears on the Canonical Central Claim.

– application
  The speech applies the Canonical Central Claim to a specific policy, market,
  institution, country, period, or event.

– evidence
  The speech mainly supplies empirical or illustrative evidence relevant to
  the Canonical Central Claim rather than independently restating its full
  thesis.

Exact semantic equivalence is not required. Topical similarity alone is
insufficient. The relationship explanation must state the meaningful
argumentative connection that justifies placement in the chapter.

canonical_ccs.json
│

canonical_themes.json
│
└── One record per Canonical Theme:
    – theme_id
    – canonical noun-phrase label
    – aliases
    – source speech IDs
    – semantic fingerprint
    – merged_from / superseded_by, where applicable

speech_classifications.json
│
└── One record per inventory speech:
    – speech_id
    – filename
    – status: categorised | uncategorised
    – classification_prompt_version
    – classification_schema_version
    – primary_cc_id, or null when uncategorised
    – primary_cc_confidence
    – primary_cc_relationship_type, exactly one controlled value when
      categorised and null when uncategorised

    – classification_rejection_reasons
    – theme_ids
    – theme_assignments
    – bucket_memberships
    – best_cc_candidates for rejected or diagnostic cases

The classification question is not whether the local central claim is a
paraphrase of the complete canonical CC. It is which supplied canonical CC
provides the speech's best argumentative home. The acceptance threshold remains
a separate numeric control; the broader relationship definition does not by
itself relax that threshold.

local_to_canonical_mappings.json
│
├── One local-CC-to-canonical-CC mapping record per speech:
│   – mapping_type: local_cc_to_canonical_cc
│   – speech_id and filename
│   – local_cc_text
│   – canonical_cc_id, or null when no accepted assignment exists
│   – confidence
│   – relationship_type
│   – relationship_explanation
│   – rationale
│   – candidate_matches
│   – classification_prompt_version
│   – classification_schema_version
│
└── Local-Theme-to-canonical-Theme mapping records retain the existing Theme
    mapping IDs, confidence, rationale, and candidate information.

The local-CC mapping must copy the accepted relationship type and explanation
from speech_classifications.json exactly. It must not infer or rewrite them.

cc_theme_pairs.json
│
└── One record per persistent Canonical CC × T pair:
    – pair_id
    – cc_id
    – theme_id
    – source_speech_ids
    – relationship_type_speech_ids
      mapping each represented controlled relationship type to the speech IDs
      carrying that role in this exact pair
    – relationship_type_counts
      deterministic counts derived from relationship_type_speech_ids
    – source_contribution_ids
    – canonical_argument_conclusion_ids
    – authoritative_anchor
    – created_at / updated_at or equivalent version metadata

The persistent CC × T pair is the authoritative many-to-many junction entity.
The runtime bucket is created from this record during assembly and is temporary.
(See Section 2, Junction Tables and Runtime Buckets, for the full definition
of both entities and how they relate.)

secondary_cc_contributions.json
│
└── One record per explicit secondary contribution, created only when
    ALLOW_SECONDARY_CC_CONTRIBUTIONS = True. When the flag is False, the file
    is an empty array; a specific source item is never mapped to a secondary
    pair regardless of what the item's content might otherwise suggest.
    – contribution_id
    – speech_id and filename
    – primary_cc_id (the speech's own default primary CC, retained for
      reference; a secondary contribution never changes this value)
    – secondary_pair_id (the Canonical CC × T pair this contribution maps
      into; must differ from every pair already reachable through the
      speech's default primary-CC memberships)
    – secondary_cc_id and secondary_theme_id
    – source_item_type: exactly one of local_argument | premise | evidence |
      rhetorical_device
    – source_item_id (the specific local argument, premise, evidence item, or
      rhetorical device this contribution is drawn from — never the whole
      speech)
    – relationship_type, drawn from the same controlled vocabulary defined in
      Notation, describing this specific item's role relative to the
      secondary CC
    – relationship_explanation, non-empty, stating why this specific item —
      not the speech as a whole — belongs to the secondary CC
    – confidence
    – rationale
    – classification_prompt_version / classification_schema_version

2. Junction Tables and Runtime Buckets

[Editorial note: this section consolidates material that was previously
scattered across the Notation, the Roles list, the ER cardinality tables, and
the Structural and Integrity Rules — the persistent Canonical CC × T pair and
the temporary runtime bucket instantiated from it are the two entities most
directly responsible for implementing the CC↔T many-to-many relationship, and
they did not previously have one place a reader could go to see them defined
together. Field-level schema for the persistent pair is defined in Section 1.D
above (cc_theme_pairs.json); this section does not repeat that schema, only
the concepts, cardinalities, and integrity rule references that govern it.]

Canonical CC × T pair
The persistent junction entity implementing the many-to-many relationship
between Canonical Central Claims and Canonical Themes. It owns the canonical
argument conclusions generated for that exact conceptual context. It exists in
cc_theme_pairs.json, has a stable pair ID, and has one authoritative
publication anchor.

Runtime CC × T bucket
A temporary Phase 5 aggregation container instantiated from one persistent
Canonical CC × T pair. It collects mapped source contributions for synthesis.
It is not text, is not part of a speech, and does not itself appear as a
publication heading. For each persistent pair, exactly one runtime bucket is
instantiated per assembly run; the bucket's validated source contributions are
grouped by controlled speech-to-chapter relationship type before any synthesis
call. The bucket input contains: pair metadata fixed by Python;
relationship_type_counts; source_contributions_by_relationship_type; and
validated source IDs, filenames, excerpts, and local argument content.

Source contribution
A provenance record linking a canonical content item to one local speech
record, local Theme, local argument, premise, evidence item, excerpt, or
rhetorical device. For primary-default placement it also carries the accepted
speech-to-CC relationship type and explanation, allowing the final synthesis
to distinguish support, mechanisms, consequences, qualifications, opposition,
historical examples, applications, and evidence. This is the entity a runtime
bucket contains and groups by relationship type.

Relevant cardinalities (full table in Section 5.A, "Entity-Relationship (ER)
Cardinalities — Table A"): C4–C7 (pair ownership of CC, Theme, and canonical
argument conclusions), C13–C16 (pair-to-bucket instantiation and bucket-to-
publication rendering), and C27 and C37 (optional secondary-pair mapping for a
specific source contribution, and its item-level granularity).

Integrity rule: see StructuralAndIntegrityRules.txt, Rule 6 ("Persistent pair
versus runtime bucket") for the full statement of what must always be true of
each entity and how they must not be conflated. That file is a reference only
and is not an input to the pipeline-construction prompt.

Secondary CC × T contribution (optional)
A second, narrower junction, active only when ALLOW_SECONDARY_CC_CONTRIBUTIONS
= True. Where the persistent Canonical CC × T pair links a whole categorised
speech to one CC × T pair by default, this junction links one specific local
argument, premise, evidence item, or rhetorical device to a Canonical CC × T
pair outside the speech's primary CC. It is disabled by default, never
inferred merely from a shared Theme, and never alters the speech's primary CC
assignment. Its persistent record schema is secondary_cc_contributions.json,
defined in Section 1.D above.


3. Nested Hierarchy Structure of Entities (=Levels) — Final compendium.md Structure Detail

[Editorial note: this section originally also contained the per-speech Phase 1
and Phase 4 pipeline record structures and the canonicalisation/assembly flow
diagram. Those are pipeline mechanics, not final-output ER structure, and have
been moved to PipelineAndIntermediateOutputs.txt. Only the final compendium.md
structure detail — which duplicates and elaborates Section 1.A above — remains
here.]

Title and Metadata

Starts with a top-level compendium title and corpus metadata, including:
– corpus description
– number of source transcripts
– date range
– generation date
– canonical model/version identifiers where available

Canonical Central Claim Chapters

Each chapter is introduced with a top-level heading:

# Central Claim: {full canonical proposition}

The next line contains the concise tagline.

Canonical Theme Sections

Within each chapter, each Canonical Theme connected to that CC is introduced
with a second-level heading:

## Theme: {canonical Theme label}

Each Theme section corresponds to exactly one persistent Canonical CC × T pair
and exactly one stable anchor.

A cross-reference note names other Canonical Central Claims under which the
same Theme appears.

Canonical Argument Conclusions

Within each Theme section, one or more canonical argument conclusions appear,
each under a third-level heading:

### Argument: {canonical conclusion}

Each canonical argument conclusion is owned by exactly one Canonical CC × T
pair and is reconstructed from one or more local argument contributions.

Every canonical argument follows a consistent substructure:

**Synthesis of premises across speeches:** – paragraph.

**Conclusion supported:** – paragraph.

**Speech-to-Chapter Relationship Roles:** – deterministic list of every
controlled relationship type represented among the canonical argument's
validated source contributions. Each role lists the source speech filenames,
source-contribution IDs, and the stored relationship explanations. This
subsection makes visible whether the argument is supported by direct support,
a specific instance, a mechanism, a consequence, a qualification, opposition,
a historical example, an application, or evidence.

**Evidence & Support:** – structured list with transcript-analysis status,
source speech, basis, and excerpt where available.

**Notable Analogies & Rhetorical Devices:** – optional structured list.

**Evolution Over Time:** – paragraph, required when the source-date span
exceeds one year.

**Provenance:** – explicit source references or machine-readable IDs.

Theme Cross-Index

After the final Canonical Central Claim chapter, compendium.md contains one
deterministically generated Theme Cross-Index for shared Themes only.

The LLM must not independently generate a second competing Theme Cross-Index.


4. Roles of the Entities (=Levels) in the Nested Hierarchy

Level / Entity                         Role in the system
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Per-speech central claim cc
The single overarching proposition inferred from one speech. It is preserved
as source-level intellectual content and mapped to zero or one primary
Canonical Central Claim.

Per-speech Theme t
A noun-phrase topic extracted from one speech. It is preserved as source-level
content and normalised to one or more Canonical Theme IDs, subject to the
speech-level limit of one to three assigned Canonical Themes.

Canonical Central Claim CC
A corpus-level, normalised, deduplicated proposition with a verb. It is the
authoritative chapter entity in compendium.md. It expresses a relationship,
causal statement, evaluative statement, prediction, or other thesis.

Speech-to-Canonical-CC relationship type
The controlled semantic role explaining how one categorised speech contributes
to its primary Canonical Central Claim chapter. It is selected during Phase 3,
preserved unchanged through Phase 4, grouped during Phase 5, and rendered in
the final canonical arguments.

Speech-to-Canonical-CC relationship explanation
A concise, non-empty explanation stating why the selected CC is the speech's
best argumentative home. It must identify an argumentative connection rather
than merely repeat a shared topic.

Canonical Theme T
A corpus-level, normalised, deduplicated noun phrase naming a topical
sub-domain. It is cross-cutting rather than owned exclusively by one CC.
It is the principal navigation entity in theme_index.md.

Canonical CC × T pair
[See Section 2, Junction Tables and Runtime Buckets, for the full definition.]

Runtime CC × T bucket
[See Section 2, Junction Tables and Runtime Buckets, for the full definition.]

Canonical Argument Conclusion
A discrete, falsifiable, deduplicated proposition reconstructed from one or
more local argument conclusions. It belongs to exactly one Canonical CC × T
pair and is the primary unit of corpus-level intellectual content beneath
that pair.

Local argument conclusion
A source-level conclusion expressed in one speech under one local Theme.
It may contribute to a canonical argument conclusion through an explicit,
provenance-preserving mapping.

Premise
A source-level logical reason supporting a local argument conclusion.
At corpus level, multiple premises are synthesised without erasing variation
or disagreement.

Synthesis of premises
Explains how source premises combine across speeches and whether the reasoning
is stable, cumulative, inconsistent, qualified, or changing.

Conclusion supported
States the exact claim established by the canonical argument and reports the
breadth of support across source speeches.

Evidence & Support
The evidentiary backbone: data points, quotations, examples, historical
precedents, anecdotes, or cited claims. Every item has exactly one
transcript-analysis status and retains source provenance.

Verification basis
Explains why an evidence item was labelled Verified, Unverifiable, or
Disputed. It prevents a status label from being interpreted as an unsupported
external fact-checking verdict.

Source contribution
[See Section 2, Junction Tables and Runtime Buckets, for the full definition.]

Notable Analogies & Rhetorical Devices
Named explanatory or persuasive devices used to make mechanisms concrete or
memorable. They remain linked to their source speeches.

Evolution Over Time
Tracks how an argument develops, sharpens, weakens, is qualified, or is
abandoned across dated speeches.

Stable anchor
A unique publication identifier for one authoritative Canonical CC × T Theme
section in compendium.md. It is the destination for index links.

Theme Cross-Index
A compact index inside compendium.md listing only Themes shared across
multiple Canonical Central Claims and linking to the relevant authoritative
sections.

Complete Theme Index
The separate theme_index.md publication view. It lists every Canonical Theme,
all associated Canonical CC × T pairs, and each pair's canonical argument
conclusions, linking back to the authoritative compendium sections.

Uncategorised speech
A speech that does not meet the required confidence threshold for assignment
to a primary Canonical Central Claim. It is logged and preserved in source
records but excluded from valid hierarchy buckets and from any
"Uncategorised Speeches" chapter.

Secondary CC contribution
An optional, explicit mapping allowing a specific speech-level argument or
source contribution to support a CC × T pair outside the speech's primary CC.
This relation is disabled by default and must never be inferred solely because
a Theme is shared across several CCs. Its persistent record schema is
secondary_cc_contributions.json, defined in Section 1.D; see also Section 2,
Junction Tables and Runtime Buckets, "Secondary CC × T contribution."

pipeline/intermediate artifact, not a final-model entity.]

6. Summary of the Authoritative Model

Source level:

Speech
→ one local central claim
→ many local Themes
→ many local arguments
→ premises, evidence, devices, unverifiable claims

Canonical level:

many local central claims
→ zero or one primary Canonical Central Claim per speech
→ exactly one controlled speech-to-CC relationship type and one non-empty
  explanation for each categorised speech

many local Themes
→ one to three Canonical Theme assignments per categorised speech

Canonical Central Claims many ↔ many Canonical Themes
→ implemented through persistent Canonical CC × T pairs

Canonical CC × T pair
→ deterministic speech-ID counts by relationship type
→ many canonical argument conclusions
→ many source contributions carrying relationship type and explanation
→ one runtime bucket per assembly run, grouped by relationship type
→ one authoritative Theme section
→ one stable anchor

Publication level:

Canonical Central Claims
→ authoritative chapters in compendium.md

Canonical Themes
→ sections under CC chapters
→ canonical arguments that visibly report Speech-to-Chapter Relationship Roles
→ complete navigation entries in theme_index.md
→ shared-theme entries in the compendium Theme Cross-Index

The same canonical data model therefore supports two complementary views:

1. Argument-oriented authoritative view:
   CC → T → canonical argument conclusions

2. Theme-oriented navigation view:
   T → CC × T pairs → canonical argument conclusions → links to compendium

The second view does not replace or duplicate the first.
